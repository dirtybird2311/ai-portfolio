## Written read

## What separated winners from losers

- **Picks vs shortlist:** The picks trailed the shortlist in 3 of 5 rebuilds (-5.53% vs -3.24%, -1.21% vs +0.38%, -7.59% vs -4.97%). They led in the other 2. The share of picks beating the shortlist median ranged from 17% to 55%, so there is no stable edge either way.
- **Repeat names:** The same names recur because the windows overlap. QNST was best in three rebuilds (+37.8% to +45.4%), and IESC was worst in four (-36.8% to -53.3%). That is closer to a few independent observations than to many.
- **Sector pattern (hypothesis):** Technology names (STX, TER, DELL, FORM, SNDK, HPE) fill many loser slots. Health Care appears on both lists.
- **Input rankings:** `l` was positive in all five rebuilds (mean +0.45, range +0.32 to +0.61), and `m` was negative in all five (mean -0.37). Both sit beyond the ±0.22 luck band every time. `e` was positive each time (mean +0.22) but is borderline. `panel_p` (mean -0.03), `composite` (-0.07) and `edge_pts` (-0.06) are inside the luck band.

## Where the judges' reasoning held up or missed

- **Held up:** The bear calls on TER (-45.6%) and SNDK (-39.1%, -22.3%) lined up with losses. The KALU thesis (estimate cuts, guidance at the low end) was followed by -28% to -31% in three rebuilds.
- **Missed:**
  - Judges were bearish or neutral on QNST in all three rebuilds where it was listed, and it was the top gainer each time.
  - The IESC thesis flagged earnings volatility but still gave "modest" odds, and the stock fell about 51% in three rebuilds.
  - Bull calls on DELL ("AI infrastructure spending") came before -39.5%.
- **Inconsistency to check:** ADAM's thesis cites a 42.1% probability, below its 50.1% base rate, because of yield exposure. Yet the judges were bullish on "10-year yield" and `panel_p` was +0.504 to +0.521. ADAM was picked in one rebuild and fell 26.3%.
- **Generic reasoning:** "Credit spreads" appears as the cited driver on very different names (FORM, CORT, HALO, MGEE, ABNB) with no visible link to outcomes.

## Hypotheses to test (each with how we would test it)

1. **`l` helps and `m` hurts.** Record both for every future rebuild and test on windows that don't overlap with these.
2. **The panel works worse over short horizons.** `panel_p` flipped to -0.29 and -0.26 in the 14-day and 4-day rebuilds. Log rank correlation at fixed horizons (5, 10 and 20 days) from every rebuild.
3. **Earnings-event names drive the big losses** (IESC, CORT). Add an earnings-within-window flag and compare returns with and without it.
4. **Macro-driver tags carry no information.** Log each judge's cited driver and compare outcomes by driver.
5. **Sector concentration explains much of the pick shortfall.** Compare picks to sector-matched shortlist names.

## What not to conclude yet

- Only five rebuilds are shown, and the windows overlap. The stated 9–14 trading days doesn't match the evidence either: windows run from 4 to 29 days.
- The picks-vs-shortlist gaps are a few points each way, within what luck gives. Passed-over names (-2.99%, -1.62%, +0.63%, -4.20%, -4.50%) barely differ from the shortlist.
- `l` and `m` are the only consistent signals, but five correlated windows are weak evidence.
- The judges are not shown to be useless, and the ADAM mismatch is one case.
- Nothing here justifies changing live settings.
