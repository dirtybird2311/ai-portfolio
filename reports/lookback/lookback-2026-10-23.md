## What separated winners from losers
- The picks trailed the shortlist in three of four rebuilds (-2.29% vs -1.96%, +1.23% vs +2.21%, -0.57% vs +0.60%). They were slightly ahead in the other (+1.75% vs +1.63%). The share of picks beating the shortlist median was 38%, 33%, 36% and 18%. These gaps are about 1 point or less, against single names ranging from -37% to +37%. That is within what luck gives.
- Nearly all the listed extremes, best and worst, were passed over. Only DXCM (-9.4%) appears as a pick.
- Repeat names drove the spread. IESC (-33.7%, -34.2%, -37.1%, -14.9%) and KALU (-28.7%, -28.6%, -30.7%, -28.1%) were among the worst in all four rebuilds. AMN was among the best in all four (+37.4%, +30.3%, +18.5%).
- Input rank correlations: `l` was positive in all four rebuilds (mean +0.31), and `m` was negative in all four (mean -0.29). Both are beyond the ±0.22 luck band. `q` averaged +0.22, but it was -0.02 in the latest rebuild. `panel_p` (+0.07), `edge_pts` (+0.07), `composite` (-0.07) and the three model probabilities (+0.07 to +0.08) are all inside luck.

## Where the judges' reasoning held up or missed
- **Held up:** the low-panel materials names (HL at 0.386 to 0.420, ALB at 0.412, NEM at 0.431) fell 8% to 19%. The bear calls on the dollar, silver and gold matched that.
- **Missed:** the KALU thesis called it a "solid setup", and it fell about 28% to 31% each time. The thesis did mention the metal-price lag normalizing, which may have been the real risk.
- **Missed:** AMN's thesis flagged volatility and poor earnings reactions, and judges said draw or bear. It was the best name three times.
- **Noise:** "bear on credit spreads" appears on both big winners (QNST, HRB, PAYC) and big losers (CORT, FORM). That call doesn't separate them.
- Panel edges were small, and some of the largest negative edges (CART -4.0, HRB -4.2) were big winners.

## Hypotheses to test (each with how we would test it)
1. **`l` carries real signal and `m` is inverted.** Log per-name `l` and `m` ranks each rebuild, then check whether the signs hold on rebuilds with non-overlapping windows.
2. **Materials and precious-metals names are systematically over-scored or under-penalised.** Tag sector at pick time and compare sector-level returns against panel_p.
3. **Repeat names dominate results.** Report each rebuild's results with IESC, KALU and AMN excluded, and count each name once.
4. **Earnings events explain the big drops.** Log the days to the next earnings date for each name and compare it with the outcome.

## What not to conclude yet
- The four rebuilds share most names and the same end date, so they are not four independent tests. The mean correlations are weaker evidence than they look.
- The latest rebuild covers only 4 trading days, so its 18% and -0.57% say almost nothing. The others cover 14 to 19 days.
- The judges are not shown to be worse than chance, and the picks are not shown to be worse than the shortlist.
- The `l` and `m` patterns are hypotheses, not causes, and nothing here supports changing live settings.
