## Written read

## What separated winners from losers
Almost every extreme name, up or down, was passed over, so the shortlist's results are driven by names we didn't hold. The picks' return versus the shortlist was mixed: -3.80% vs -1.75%, +6.37% vs -0.35%, +1.75% vs +1.46%, +0.88% vs -2.19%, -1.91% vs -2.45%. The share of picks beating the shortlist median was 38%, 50%, 45%, 55% and 33%, which is a coin flip.

Of the inputs, `l` (+0.45 mean, +0.35 to +0.62 per rebuild) and `e` (+0.35, +0.29 to +0.40) sit above the ±0.22 luck band in every rebuild. `m` is negative in all five (-0.12 to -0.28), though only some are outside the band. `panel_p` (+0.12) and `composite` (-0.09) are within luck.

## Where the judges' reasoning held up or missed
- **Held up:** Bearish or cautious calls on TER, KALU, HL, IOSP and OTTR preceded falls. VCTR (bull) rose in both rebuilds where it was picked.
- **Missed:** Bear calls on QNST, SN, HRB, CART and TPC preceded gains of +20% to +34%. Bull on DELL preceded -36.5%.
- **Generic rationales:** "Credit spreads" appears as a bear, draw or bull reason on names with opposite outcomes (QNST +33.6%, BKH +24.3%, CORT -36.9%, VCTR +16.3%).
- **ADAM:** It was picked in the 10-05 rebuild (-32.0%), though its thesis text gives a 42.1% probability favoring underperformance while the label reads "bull." That mismatch is worth checking.
- **Thin probabilities:** `panel_p` runs only about 0.39 to 0.53, so the judges rarely express strong conviction.

## Hypotheses to test (each with how we would test it)
1. **`l` and `e` carry the ranking signal.** Log their rank correlations on each new rebuild and check whether they stay outside ±0.22. Also test whether a shortlist built from them alone beats the current picks.
2. **`m` works against us.** Track it for several more rebuilds and compare returns of high-`m` vs low-`m` names within the shortlist.
3. **Judges underrate rate-sensitive and high-momentum names (TPC, QNST, REGN).** Tag each bear call by theme and compare the hit rate against bull calls.
4. **Macro-theme rationales are boilerplate.** Count how often one theme (e.g., credit spreads) is cited for unrelated names and whether citing it predicts anything.
5. **Picks vs. shortlist gap.** Record pick-minus-shortlist return per rebuild alongside the median-beat share to see whether the average gap is driven by a few names.

## What not to conclude yet
- The windows run 9 to 34 trading days, longer than the 9 to 14 days the framework assumes. The rebuilds overlap and share names (REGN, QNST, IESC, ADAM, DELL appear repeatedly), so five rebuilds are not five independent tests.
- Consistent signs across rebuilds are weaker evidence than they look, because the same stocks drive them.
- Per-model correlations are mostly within luck. Kimi's +0.29 comes from one rebuild only, and several models have n/a entries.
- Picks beating the shortlist on average return in four of five rebuilds, with median-beat shares at 33% to 55%, is consistent with luck.
- A handful of -35% to -50% moves (IESC, STX, TER, DELL) can swamp any group average.
- Nothing here supports changing live settings. It only shows what to log next.
